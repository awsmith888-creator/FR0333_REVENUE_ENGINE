# STUDY.0001 . Psalm 23:1–4

## Passage and textual layer

The passage reference follows S.2. S.5 supplies a modern Hebrew witness, not an autograph. Its opening divine-name/shepherd phrase is **יְהוָה רֹעִי**; explanatory English gloss: **“The LORD is my shepherd.”** This gloss is not presented as a quotation from an Ethiopian English Bible. No KJV text is used as the base.

Working paraphrase of the four verses: the speaker trusts God for provision, rest, renewed life and right guidance; even in dangerous darkness, God's presence and shepherding implements bring reassurance. This is an explanatory summary checked against S.5, not a new canonical translation.

## Historical and literary context

The Hebrew heading associates the poem with David. That heading does not independently establish a composition year or an incident in his life. Its pastoral setting uses care and guidance familiar from tending a flock. Notice the literary movement from speaking about God to directly addressing God in verse 4 [S.5].

Ethiopian reception belongs to a transmitted scriptural and worship tradition. S.4 discusses uncertainty over when particular Ethiopic translations were made. A date for Christianity's arrival alone cannot date every translated book. No precise translation date is assigned to this Psalm here.

## Ethiopian Orthodox interpretive layer

The 2014 Ledeta LeMariam lesson connects this shepherd imagery to Christ's saving care and sacramental life [S.2 / PDF pages 17–19]. Its symbolic use of the staff and Cross is a Christian reading, not a claim that the Hebrew noun literally means Cross. The lesson is attributable parish instruction; a verse-specific Andemta witness has not yet been recovered.

S.3 supplies context for the Eucharist's importance in Ethiopian worship. That context does not turn a liturgical English translation into the original wording of this Psalm.

## Evidence boundary

Exact Ge'ez wording and its edition-specific reference are HOLD. The lesson names no English Bible edition; its quotation is not silently adopted. S.6 and S.7 are manuscript recovery leads only. We have not inspected their Psalm folios.
